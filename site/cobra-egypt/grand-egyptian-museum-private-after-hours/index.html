Field guide · 06 of 20

Can you visit the Grand Egyptian Museum after hours or privately?

Short answer

Public hours are 9am–6pm daily, with late opening to 9pm on Wednesdays and Saturdays as of 2026. Private guided visits during those hours are straightforward. Visits after public closing are arranged case by case through the museum's group-booking channel and licensed partners; they are not a ticket you can buy online. We arrange after-hours access where available.

What the Grand Egyptian Museum is

The Grand Egyptian Museum sits on the edge of the Giza Plateau, about two kilometres from the pyramids, and opened fully to the public on 4 November 2025 after a partial opening a year earlier. It covers more than 500,000 square metres and holds over 100,000 objects across twelve main galleries, which makes it the largest museum devoted to a single civilisation.

Three things define a first visit. The atrium, where the 82-tonne statue of Ramesses II stands beneath a hanging obelisk. The Grand Staircase, 148 steps lined with royal statuary that climbs to a window framing the pyramids. And the Tutankhamun galleries, which for the first time show the full contents of the tomb, more than 5,000 objects, in one place. The two cedar boats of Khufu, moved here from their old shelter beside the Great Pyramid, have their own building within the complex and are included in the ticket.

Tickets are sold online for a dated, timed slot through the official site; on-site sales have been suspended at times, so book ahead. Foreign adult admission was in the region of EGP 1,450 without a guide and EGP 1,950 with the museum's own group tour at the 2025 opening; children under six are free. Check current rules. Personal photography without flash is allowed, and in parts of the Tutankhamun galleries only phones are permitted; tripods, selfie sticks and live streaming are not.

What "private" means at the GEM

There are three different things people mean, and they are worth separating.

A private guided visit in public hours. This is simply you, your Egyptologist and a timed ticket. Your guide walks you through in the order that makes sense for your interests rather than the museum's group route. It is always available and is how most of our Cairo days begin or end.

An evening visit. Wednesdays and Saturdays run to 9pm. The galleries after 6pm are markedly quieter than at 11am, the atrium is lit, and the staircase window shows the pyramids against a dark sky. This needs no special arrangement beyond the right day.

After-hours access. A visit after the public has left, with the galleries open for your party alone, is handled by the museum through its corporate and travel-agency channel and executed with a licensed partner. Terms, availability and fees are set by the museum and change. It is not advertised as a product and is not available every night. We arrange it where available, and we say clearly in your plan whether it has been confirmed or is still requested.

The museum also runs its own after-closing immersive show, "Eternal Sun", as a separate ticketed add-on. It is a projection experience, not gallery access, and should not be confused with a private visit.

What to see in three hours

With a guide and three hours, this is the order we use.

  1. Atrium and Ramesses II (20 minutes). Stand back; the scale is the point.
  2. Grand Staircase (30 minutes). Kings, queens and gods arranged by theme up the climb, with the pyramids at the top. The moving walkway alongside is there if the steps are a problem.
  3. Tutankhamun galleries (90 minutes). Entered from gallery 7, five sections: discovery, lifestyle, rebirth, funeral, identity. The museum suggests two hours; ninety with an Egyptologist who knows which cases to pass is enough on a first visit.
  4. One period gallery (30 minutes). Old Kingdom if you are going to Saqqara, New Kingdom if you are heading to Luxor. Your guide chooses.
  5. Khufu's boats (optional, 30–45 minutes). A separate building; worth it if you have the time and will not see the pyramid plateau the same day.

Skip the gift shops until the end, and take the coffee break on the terrace, which also faces the plateau.

Pairing it with the pyramids

The GEM and the Giza Plateau are neighbours, and the new plateau visitor centre is beside the museum. Most visitors try to do both in one day. We recommend against it on a first visit: the plateau in the early morning and the museum the next afternoon, or the museum on a late-opening evening after a rest at the hotel. If the two must share a day, pyramids at opening, lunch, museum from 2pm.

Our approach

Your Egyptologist is with you for the whole trip, so the museum is not a stand-alone visit; it is the key to the sites you see later. We book the timed tickets, choose the day for light and crowds, and request after-hours access where available for travellers who want the galleries to themselves.

FAQ

Can you buy Grand Egyptian Museum tickets at the door? Not reliably. As of 2026 tickets are sold online for a dated, timed slot through the official site, and on-site sales have been suspended at times. Book before you travel, through the official site or your operator.

How long do you need at the Grand Egyptian Museum? Three hours with a guide covers the atrium, the Grand Staircase, the Tutankhamun galleries and one period gallery. A full day lets you add Khufu's boats and a second gallery. Two days sees almost everything.

Is photography allowed at the GEM? Yes, for personal use, without flash, tripods or selfie sticks. In designated parts of the Tutankhamun galleries only phone photography is permitted, and some areas are no-photo. Check signage on the day.
