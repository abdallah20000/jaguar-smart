Is Egypt safe for tourists in 2026?

Short answer

Yes, for the places tourists actually go. As of late 2026, the US State Department rates Egypt Level 2 ("exercise increased caution") and the UK FCDO advises against travel only to North Sinai, the Libyan border and parts of the Western Desert. Cairo, Giza, Luxor, Aswan and the Nile are outside those zones. The everyday problem is hassle, not violence.

What the official advisories actually say

Two advisories matter most to our travellers. The US State Department keeps Egypt at Level 2, "Exercise Increased Caution", and said in March 2026 that its overall assessment had not changed even as it raised several neighbouring countries to Level 3. The UK FCDO, updated 27 October 2026, advises against all travel to North Sinai and the strip within 20 km of the Libyan border, and against all but essential travel to northern South Sinai, Ismailia east of the Suez Canal and most of the Western Desert.

Read the exceptions carefully. The FCDO specifically carves out Luxor, Qena, Aswan, Abu Simbel and the Valley of the Kings, the Fayoum, Siwa, and the Bahariya, Farafra, Dakhla and Kharga oases with the White and Black Deserts. In other words, the classic private itinerary (Cairo, Giza, Saqqara, Luxor, a Nile sailing, Aswan, Abu Simbel) sits entirely outside the warning zones.

Both governments note that the wider Middle East is unpredictable and that the situation can change at short notice. That is true. It is also why we watch the advisories daily and re-route if a page changes.

Violent crime versus everyday hassle

The honest distinction is this. Violent crime against foreign visitors in Egypt's tourist areas is rare. Petty theft happens, as it does in any large city, and is mostly opportunistic: an unattended phone, a bag left on a café chair.

What wears people down is the hassle. Unsolicited "guides" at the Giza entrance. A camel ride that was free to start and expensive to end. Taxi meters that do not run. A shopkeeper who walks you into his cousin's papyrus gallery. None of this is dangerous. All of it is tiring, and it is the main reason first-time visitors come home saying they would not return.

The practical defence is simple: arrive with a licensed guide and a driver who are already paid, so there is nothing for anyone else to sell you. That single change removes most of the friction.

Desert roads, checkpoints and escorts

Egypt keeps a visible security presence around tourist sites and on intercity roads. Expect checkpoints on the road from Aswan to Abu Simbel and on desert highways, and expect your passport to be checked. On some desert routes the authorities still require vehicles to travel at set times or with a police escort; a licensed operator arranges this before you leave the hotel. The tourist police at sites are there for you, not against you. If you are travelling independently, avoid driving yourself at night outside cities.

Solo women and couples

Women travelling alone report verbal harassment in crowded streets and markets far more often than any physical threat. The uncomfortable moments cluster in the same places as the hassle: downtown Cairo pavements, Khan el-Khalili, the approaches to the pyramids. With a guide at your side those moments largely disappear. Dressing modestly (shoulders and knees covered) reduces attention in non-resort areas and is simply good manners in mosques. A woman guide can be requested; we arrange one where available.

Health, water and the small risks

Tap water is not for drinking; bottled water is everywhere. Stomach upsets are the most common complaint and are usually food-hygiene related, not serious. Sun and heat are the real physical risks from April to October: Luxor and Aswan regularly exceed 40 °C in summer. Travel insurance that covers Egypt is essential, and travelling against FCDO advice would invalidate most UK policies, which is another reason to stay inside the exceptions above.

Our view

Egypt is a country of more than one hundred million people where the tourist corridor is heavily protected and the rest is a normal, busy, sometimes chaotic place. If you stay within the corridor, travel with a licensed guide and a known driver, and let one person manage the moving parts, the honest answer to "is Egypt safe" is yes. The remaining risk is regional and political, and it is the one we monitor for you.

FAQ

Is Cairo safe at night? Central Cairo, Zamalek, Garden City and Maadi are busy late into the night and generally fine for walking in lit, populated streets. Use a hotel-arranged car rather than hailing taxis, and avoid demonstrations or large gatherings.

Is Egypt safe for solo female travellers? Yes, with the caveat that verbal attention in crowded areas is common. Most women find it tiring rather than threatening. A private guide, modest dress outside resorts and pre-booked transport remove most of it.

Do I need a police escort to visit Abu Simbel? Not for the standard day visit by road or by the 45-minute flight from Aswan. There are checkpoints on the road. Any route that still requires timed travel or an escort is arranged by your operator in advance.
