Is Abu Simbel worth it, and how do you get there from Aswan?

Short answer

Yes. Abu Simbel is the most complete monument of Ramses II and the one Nubian site that justifies a full day. From Aswan you either fly (about 45 minutes each way) or drive 280 km (3.5–4 hours each way). Allow 2–3 hours at the temples. The sun aligns with the inner sanctuary on 22 February and 22 October.

Why Abu Simbel is worth the detour

Abu Simbel sits 280 km south of Aswan, close to the Sudanese border, on the shore of Lake Nasser. There are two temples: the Great Temple of Ramses II, with its four seated colossi over 20 metres high, and the smaller temple of Hathor dedicated to Queen Nefertari.

What sets it apart is scale and intent. The temple was cut into a sandstone cliff, then, in the 1960s, cut into blocks and lifted 65 metres to escape the rising lake. The relocation is visible if you know where to look. Your Egyptologist will show you the seams and the concrete dome behind the façade.

For a traveller who has already seen Karnak and Philae, the question is fair: is one more temple worth a long day? Our answer is yes, with one condition. Go at the right hour. Before 9am the forecourt is quiet and the light hits the colossi from the side. By late morning the day-trip coaches have arrived.

Fly or drive: an honest comparison

A few notes from 2026. The police convoy that once governed the road is no longer mandatory; drivers pass checkpoints every 60–80 km instead. Flights run most days but not every day, and the schedule shifts seasonally. In January 2026 flights were suspended for a day because of a sandstorm, so a drive remains the dependable fallback.

For most of our travellers over 45 we recommend the flight for a day visit and the drive only if you want to stay overnight.

The overnight option

Abu Simbel town has a small number of lodges on the lake. Sleeping there changes the visit. You see the temples at the end of the afternoon after the coaches have gone, watch the sound-and-light show from an almost empty forecourt, and return at opening time the next morning with the site to yourselves. You then drive or fly back to Aswan by lunchtime.

This is the version we build into most private itineraries that have a day to spare. The other, for those with more time, is a Lake Nasser cruise of three or four nights that ends at the temple jetty.

The Sun Festival, 22 February and 22 October

Twice a year the rising sun travels 60 metres through the temple and lights three of the four statues in the inner sanctuary: Ramses II, Ra-Horakhty and Amun. Ptah, god of the underworld, stays in shadow. In 2026 the dates are Sunday 22 February and Thursday 22 October, with the light entering at roughly 6:35–6:45am and lasting a few minutes.

It is a remarkable thing to witness, and it is crowded: several thousand people queue in the dark for a brief view. The alignment is nearly as good on the day before and after, with fewer visitors. If the festival matters to you, tell us early. Flights and lodges around those dates sell out months ahead.

Tickets, hours and practicalities

  • Opening hours: daily, 6am to 5pm, as of 2026.
  • Foreign adult ticket: typically EGP 600–750 (about USD 12–15); roughly double on Sun Festival days. Check current prices at the official ticketing site before you go.
  • Photography permits for professional equipment cost extra.
  • There is little shade. Bring water, a hat and sunglasses even in winter.
  • Allow 1.5 hours for the two temples at a brisk pace, 2–3 hours with your Egyptologist and a walk to the lake view.

We book the flights, position a driver for the overnight version, and time the visit for the quietest hour we can get.

FAQ

Can you do Abu Simbel in a day from Aswan? Yes. By air it is a half day: a 6–7am departure, 3–4 hours on site, back in Aswan by early afternoon. By road it is a full day with a 4:30am start and a return around 2–3pm.

Is the Abu Simbel Sun Festival worth planning a trip around? Only if the event itself matters to you. It is crowded and brief. The day before or after gives almost the same alignment with fewer people. For the temple itself, any early morning is better.

How much does Abu Simbel cost to enter? As of 2026 a foreign adult ticket is typically EGP 600–750, about USD 12–15, and higher on Sun Festival days. Flights, a private driver and your guide are separate. Check the official ticketing portal for current rates.
